1The problem

Fire doesn't behave the same way in space

On Earth, hot air rises and pulls fresh oxygen into a flame. In orbit nothing rises, so a fire lives or dies by the cabin fans. On the Moon and Mars, gravity comes back, but only partly. This story follows one flame from NASA's flight tests to a whole spacecraft.

    5What we still don't know

    The next tests matter more than the next model

    The ship score rests on placeholder weights, and the Moon and Mars answers rest on predictions. These gaps are where new data would change the story most.

    No partial-gravity fire tests

    All 38 tests here were in microgravity. NASA's planned FM2 experiment is designed to burn materials on the Moon for the first time.

    Nothing above 31% oxygen

    The proposed Moon-base air is 34% oxygen. The closest flight tests stopped at about 31%, and they kept burning.

    Placeholder ship weights

    Flammability points and factor weights need replacing with real test results, such as NASA-STD-6001 data.
